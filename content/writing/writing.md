UNION PHOTO CO. BRAND VOICE GUIDE

1. Voice in one sentence

Union Photo Co. sounds like a Seattle creative friend who knows weddings and luxury events cold, talks to you like a real person, backs every claim with specifics, and skips the brochure language.

2. Tone pillars

Friendly and grounded
- Write like you are talking to someone over coffee, not pitching them. Lead with the point, keep it warm.
- Strong opener example: "Let's be honest, most trade show booths are a snooze." Easy, conversational, true.
- Skip the brochure register. Weak opener to avoid: "we take a look at the crème de la crème of photo booths that promise to add a timeless charm to your occasions." Read out loud, it sounds like a press release.

Specific and proof-led
- Use numbers, names, places, outcomes. Specifics build trust faster than adjectives.
- Strong examples: "we capture 80 opt-in emails per hour" and "85 percent of guests complete the in-booth survey."
- Vague phrases like "studio-quality images taken by real photographers" float and prove nothing. Say which photographer, which venue, which booth model.

Useful before promotional
- The article should earn its keep in advice before any pitch. Bad pattern: collapsing into self-promotion by paragraph three with phrases like "Why Settle for Less?" Teach first, sell at the end, and only after you have given the reader something to take with her.

Warm without small talk
- Warmth lives in the details, not the salutation. Skip "Hi everyone, hope you're staying warm and cozy this week!" That intro adds nothing.
- Open with a real moment instead: the song that pulled grandma onto the dance floor, the line of bridesmaids waiting for prints, the look the couple shared while the booth was running. Specific is warm. Generic is not.

3. Sentence and paragraph mechanics

- Sentence length target: 10 to 18 words, average around 14. Mix one short sentence into every three or four longer ones for rhythm. Fragments are fine for emphasis, like "Easy. Done."
- Avoid dense prose at 22 to 28 words per sentence with three to five sentences stacked together. It reads heavy on mobile, where most brides are scrolling.
- Paragraph length target: 2 to 4 sentences. One-sentence paragraphs allowed when you want a moment to land.
- Read every section out loud. If you run out of breath, cut.

4. Hook library, 5 reusable openings

Hook A, the reader's question
- Template: "What do you do when [her real planning tension]?"
- Example: "What do you do when your venue is gorgeous but your guest list keeps growing?"

Hook B, the gentle contrarian
- Template: "Most [thing in the category] [common claim]. Here is the truth."
- Example: "Most photo booths promise fun, but fall flat the second the night starts to drag."

Hook C, the named feeling
- Template: "If you have [done X], you already know [feeling]."
- Example: "If you have started planning a Seattle wedding, you already know how fast the vendor list gets long."

Hook D, the scene
- Template: Open with one sensory detail from the actual event. Name the venue, the moment, the song, the prop. Skip the salutation.
- Bad example to avoid: "Today we are sharing images from [Couple]'s elegant wedding at [Venue]." Better version names a single image or moment first, then zooms out.

Hook E, retire the cliche
- Template: "[Industry cliche] is over. Here is what couples are loving now."
- Example: "The dusty prop trunk is over. Here is what couples are loving now."

5. Vocabulary

Use column
- Plain nouns: photo booth, prints, backdrop, attendant, venue, wedding planner, couple, guest, gallery, frame, lighting, props, scrapbook, guestbook
- Seattle specifics: PNW, Pacific Northwest, Capitol Hill, Ballard, SODO, Bainbridge, Olympic Sculpture Park, Fairmont Olympic, MOHAI, Block 41
- Warm, plain verbs that show motion: shoot, print, set up, light, design, build, ship, run, capture, hand over, hang, line up
- Soft connectors that keep things human: honestly, the truth is, here is the thing, the short version, what we love about
- Hard numbers: hours of coverage, prints per hour, square feet, lead time in weeks, count of attendants

Avoid column
- Filler luxe vocabulary: elevate, transcendent, timeless, bespoke, artisanal, sophisticated, exquisite, discerning, crème de la crème, silent champion, magic touch
- Hype filler: revolutionize, game-changer, paradigm shift, seamless, frictionless, next-generation, scalable, leverage, synergy, ecosystem, deep dive, holistic
- Vague brand puffery: "studio-grade," "premium experience," "elevated narrative," "lasting impact." Replace each with a concrete fact.
- Em dashes. Use commas, periods, semicolons.
- Emoji and hashtags inside body copy.
- Patronizing terms for the reader: "discerning couples," "ladies," "sweetie." Talk to her like an equal who happens to be planning something big.

6. Structure templates

A. Planning advice for couples, 700 to 1,100 words
- Hook A or B that names a real planning question, e.g. "What should a photo booth actually cost for a 120-guest Seattle wedding?"
- The short answer in 2 to 3 sentences near the top, so readers who bounce still walk away with something useful
- Three to five subsections, each a real decision she will make, with a concrete recommendation and a price or time range
- One short real-world example with venue and date if you have it
- Local SEO anchor sentence, e.g. "For weddings in the Seattle area, plan a 6 by 8 foot footprint near the bar."
- CTA: soft availability check

B. Real wedding or event feature, 500 to 800 words
- Hook D, one image and one detail. Skip "Hi everyone."
- Credits block early: couple or host, venue, planner, photographer, florist, caterer. Use real names and link out.
- Two to four short paragraphs on what made the night work, written like notes from the team that ran the booth
- Specific moments: a song, a prop, a guest count, a line length, a print finish, the toast that made everyone cry
- Three to six selected images with captions that name the moment
- CTA: invite the reader to share her own date

C. Vendor or venue spotlight, 600 to 900 words
- Hook C that names what the vendor solves for her
- One paragraph on who the vendor is and what they own (style, range, price band)
- A short specifics block: years operating, team size, geographic range, signature work
- Two to three short anecdotes from events worked together. One should include a real problem and how the vendor solved it.
- A short side block for couples: "Best fit if you want X. Not the right call if Y."
- CTA: direct ask to connect Union with the planner or venue

7. CTA patterns, 3 examples

- Direct ask, B2B leaning: "Planning a Seattle wedding and want a photo booth that actually fits your design? Send your date and venue. We will get back to you within one business day."
- Soft availability check, couples leaning: "Curious if your date is open? Drop it here and we will check."
- Partner pitch, vendor leaning: "Planners and venues, if you want a booth that respects your design, here is our trade kit." Pair with a concrete promise, e.g. response within one business day or a deck attached.

8. Do not list

- Do not open a real wedding post with a weather note or a "Hi everyone." It reads as filler.
- Do not stack adjectives like "elegant, sophisticated, timeless, classy." Pick one specific noun instead.
- Do not put the brand name three times in the first 150 words. It reads as insecurity.
- Do not write "you get what you pay for" or any other cliche dressed up as wisdom.
- Do not use em dashes, hashtags inside paragraphs, emoji, or all-caps headers.
- Do not make claims you cannot back with a number, a name, or a photo.
- Do not close with "let the magic unfold" or anything in that register.
- Do not call her "discerning," "ladies," or "sweetie." Talk to her like an adult who happens to be planning a wedding.

9. Pre-publish checklist

- Hook earns the next paragraph. Cut anything that does not.
- Every section has at least one number, name, or place.
- Sentence average is 10 to 18 words. Read it out loud.
- The piece sounds like a person talking, not a brand broadcasting.
- No em dashes, no emoji, no hashtags in body.
- No words from the avoid column above.
- Seattle or PNW reference appears in the title, the first 200 words, or a section header.
- One photo with a caption that names the moment, not just the couple.
- CTA is specific, with a time promise or a clear next step.
- One internal link to a service page and one external link to a vendor, venue, or source.
- Final pass: cut the weakest paragraph. The piece will be better.
