Most photo booth listings sound the same. Here is what actually matters when you are picking one for a Seattle wedding, corporate event, or birthday.

The short version: for a 120-guest wedding, plan on 4 hours of coverage, an open-air booth, a staffed attendant, and a 6 by 8 foot footprint near the bar. Budget 1,200 to 2,000 dollars in the Seattle area for a quality setup. Everything else is a detail you can make smart calls on.

### 1. Match the Booth to the Event

Different events need different setups. The booth you put at a Capitol Hill cocktail launch is not the booth you want for a Bainbridge barn wedding.

- **Weddings.** Open-air booth, custom print template with the couple's names and date, classic prop box. Skip the LED-lit shells.
- **Corporate events.** Branded backdrop, logo on every print, digital sharing first so prints are a bonus. Lead capture is the real win here.
- **Birthdays and social parties.** GIF and Boomerang stations, full prop kit, faster turnover. Less template fuss, more energy.

### 2. Open-Air vs. Enclosed

Open-air booths are what we run at almost every event. They fit groups of 8 to 10 guests, work with any backdrop, and photograph well under standard venue lighting.

Enclosed booths feel nostalgic. They cap at 4 guests, take up more floor space, and need their own power and ventilation. Pick enclosed only if the room calls for it.

### 3. Print Quality Is Not Negotiable

The prints are what guests take home, so they need to last. Ask three questions:

- Is the printer dye-sublimation, not inkjet?
- How long is the print cycle? Under 15 seconds is the bar.
- Are prints water-resistant?

Dye-sub prints last decades and survive a drink spill. Inkjet smudges and fades. There is no reason to pay for a booth that prints on an inkjet.

### 4. Digital Sharing Should Be Standard

Modern booths send photos to your phone the moment you tap a screen. Confirm the booth includes:

- Text and email delivery
- A QR code gallery for the full event
- Animated GIFs and Boomerangs

If a vendor charges extra for digital sharing, you are paying 2018 prices.

### 5. Staffed, Not Drop-Off

A staffed attendant runs the booth, manages the prop table, and reprints when guests want extras. We have never seen a self-service setup go well at a 100-plus guest event. The line gets long, props go missing, and someone ends up troubleshooting the printer instead of dancing.

Make sure an attendant is included, not an upsell.

### 6. What the Package Should Cover

A fair Seattle quote at 1,200 to 2,000 dollars for 4 hours should include:

- Unlimited sessions during the event window
- Unlimited prints
- A custom print template designed to your event
- An online gallery with downloadable photos
- One trained attendant on site

If any of those are listed as add-ons, the base price is misleading.

### A Footprint Note for Seattle Venues

For weddings in the Seattle area, plan a 6 by 8 foot footprint near the bar or dance floor. Olympic Sculpture Park, Block 41, and MOHAI all have natural spots for an open-air setup. Talk to your planner before you sign anything.
